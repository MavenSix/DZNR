Opening

Watch For · Recovery Moves
Someone tries to dominate

Someone barely speaks

Heavy disagreement breaks out

Someone asks for slides

ACTIVITY 2
Product Vision Workshop ⏱ 0:35 – 1:25 · 50 min · 🤝 Human + AI
▾
Goal: Build a shared product vision using 7 structured prompts. Surface what's in scope and what's explicitly out.
📋 Materials
  • Sticky notes (3 colors)
  • Sharpies
  • Wall space for the 7 prompts
  • Camera for capturing board
🤖 AI Agent in Play
Agent 04 · Vision Clustering
Trigger: When stickies are captured (10 min before activity ends)
Step-by-Step
0:35
(3 min) DS introduces 7 prompts: FOR · WHAT · IS A TOOL FOR · SO THAT · UNLIKE · OUR PRODUCT WILL · BUT IT DOESN'T
0:38
(12 min) Silent sticky generation. 1–3 stickies per prompt per person. Stickies up on board as they go.
0:50
(15 min) Walk-through — DS reads stickies cluster by cluster, asking "any reactions?" after each.
1:05
(5 min) Tech Lead displays Agent 04 output: 3 candidate vision statements + heat map.
1:10
(10 min) Group selects, edits, confirms ONE vision statement. DS reads back. Group nods or pushes back.
1:20
(5 min) DS captures all "BUT IT DOESN'T" stickies as Won't Have seed list for Day 2.
Facilitator Script
Opening
"There's a structure called Geoffrey Moore's product vision template — we're using it to give us a shared mental model. Seven prompts on the board. You write 1–3 stickies per prompt, in silence, no second-guessing. We're looking for raw material, not polished thoughts."
During silent generation
(Walk the room. Stay quiet. If someone is staring at a blank prompt, just say: "What would your boss say?" — don't answer for them.)
When AI shows 3 vision drafts
"The AI took everything you wrote and produced three candidate vision statements. None of these are right. They're a starting point for us to react to. Let's read each one and tell me what you'd change."
Watch For · Recovery Moves
"BUT IT DOESN'T" prompt has < 3 stickies
"Out-of-scope is critical. Each of you, name one thing this is NOT."
Vision statements feel too vague
Pick the most specific draft. "This one feels closest. What words are wrong?"
Disagreement between two vision drafts
"Let's try a hybrid. What if we said [combine A's subject + B's differentiator]?"
Someone wants to argue scope
Capture as Won't Have. "We're not deciding scope yet — we're cataloging."
📦 Output: Validated Product Vision Statement. Won't Have seed list. Consensus heat map for Day 2.

Group can't agree on persona's goal

How Might We + Voting ⏱ 2:40 – 3:40 · 60 min · 🤝 Human + AI
▾
Goal: Generate HMW questions, cluster with AI, vote on priorities. End Day 1 with the top problems to solve.
📋 Materials
  • Sticky notes (different color from Vision)
  • Sharpies
  • 5 dot stickers per person
  • Whiteboard for affinity map
🤖 AI Agents in Play
Agents 06 + 07 · HMW Affinity + Voting
Trigger: Agent 06 when HMWs are written. Agent 07 after voting.
Step-by-Step
2:40
(3 min) DS introduces HMW format: "How might we [verb] [for whom] [outcome]?" — one example on the board.
2:43
(12 min) Silent HMW generation. Each person writes 3–5 HMWs, focused on validated personas' frustrations.
2:55
(5 min) Stickies up on the wall. Tech Lead photographs board, feeds to Agent 06.
3:00
(8 min) Agent 06 produces 4–6 themed clusters in ~3 min. DS reads cluster names aloud, group validates.
3:08
(5 min) Outliers: "These don't fit any cluster. Standalone or trash?"
3:13
(7 min) Voting: 5 dot stickers each. Vote on clusters that matter most. "Vote with your priorities, not for what you think we should build."
3:20
(5 min) Agent 07 tallies votes. DS reads top 3 cluster results aloud.
3:25
(10 min) Co-author Problem Statement: "[Persona] needs [need] because [insight], but currently [obstacle]." DS writes live.
3:35
(5 min) Read final Problem Statement aloud. Confirm verbal agreement from each participant.
Facilitator Script
Opening
"How Might We questions are the bridge from problem to solution. Each one starts with 'How might we...' — open enough to invite ideas, anchored in your personas' real frustrations. We're not solving yet. We're framing the problems we want to solve."
Before voting
"This vote is about priority, not solution. You're not voting for what we should build. You're voting for what matters most to solve. Five dots each. Spread them or stack them — your call."
Closing Problem Statement
"Read it back to me. Does this capture what we're here to solve? If anyone has even a 5% disagreement, say it now — tomorrow we build a spec from this."
Watch For · Recovery Moves
HMWs are too solution-y
"Strip out the solution. 'How might we add chat' becomes 'How might we help users get answers faster.'"
Cluster labels are wrong
Take 30 sec — relabel with the group: "What unifies these? What's the root question?"
Voting concentrates on one person's passion
Note privately. In Day 2: "Was that genuine consensus or [name]'s strong feelings?"
Problem Statement is generic
"Add who. Add why. Specifics anchor the spec tomorrow."
📦 Output: Top 3 priority clusters. Agreed Problem Statement. Persona-to-priority mapping. Day 2 prep package.

ACTIVITY 5 ⭐
MoSCoW + Live Spec Matrix ⏱ 0:05 – 1:20 · 75 min · 🤝 Human + AI
▾
Goal: Take feature hypotheses from Day 1 and convert them into a prioritized, sized, dependency-mapped Spec Matrix the dev team can build from.
📋 Materials
  • Two screens: Spec Matrix on main, facilitator slides on second
  • Printed feature hypothesis list from Day 1 synthesis
  • Sticky notes for new ideas
🤖 AI Agent in Play
Agent 09 ⭐ · MoSCoW Spec Matrix (most critical)
Trigger: Active throughout — updates Spec Matrix live as group decides
Step-by-Step
0:05
(5 min) DS introduces MoSCoW: Must / Should / Could / Won't. "Must = product fails without it. Should = important but not critical. Could = nice-to-have. Won't = explicitly out of scope."
0:10
(5 min) Tech Lead displays Agent 09 pre-populated Spec Matrix on screen — features from Day 1 with AI-suggested MoSCoW tiers and reasoning.
0:15
(45 min) Walk through every feature row by row. SA gives feasibility, group decides M/S/C/W, AI updates live. Watch dependency conflicts. Hold ~1.5 min/feature average.
1:00
(10 min) Capacity check: total Must Have story points vs. sprint capacity. If over, group descopes. AI flags candidates.
1:10
(10 min) DS reads final Must Haves aloud. Group confirms. Reads Won't Haves aloud — "explicit out-of-scope. Anyone want to challenge?"
Facilitator Script
Opening
"Now we're prioritizing. MoSCoW is simple: Must Have means the product literally does not work without this. Should is important but not critical. Could is nice. Won't means we're explicitly NOT building it now — but we're writing it down so it doesn't come back as scope creep. The AI on screen has pre-suggested tiers based on what you said yesterday. We're going to walk through each feature and you'll override the AI wherever you disagree."
When debating tier
"Three things to consider: Does the product fail without this? Does it serve a Must persona? Are we sure we can ship without it? If two yeses, it's a Must."
When over capacity
"We have more Musts than fit in the sprint. Two options: descope a Must to Should, or extend timeline. Which?"
Closing
"Read it back. [Reads Must Haves and Won't Haves.] This is what we're building. This is what we're not. Last call to challenge — speak now."
Watch For · Recovery Moves
Everything is becoming a Must
"If everything is critical, nothing is. Pick the top 5. Justify each."
SA blocks a Must on feasibility
"What's the technical concern? Can we descope to make it feasible, or do we need a different approach?"
AI suggestion is rejected
"Good — humans decide. Tech Lead, capture the override reason. AI updates."
Dependency conflict
"Feature B requires Feature A. A is currently Could. Either A becomes Must, or B descopes. Which?"
Stakeholder pushes pet feature
Validate respectfully. "Walk me through who this serves and what breaks without it."
📦 Output: Complete MoSCoW Spec Matrix with story points, ACs for Must Haves, dependency map. Machine-readable JSON.

📋 Materials

🤖 AI Agent in Play
Agent 10 · Journey & Gap Analysis
Trigger: Drafts journey scaffold pre-activity; gap analysis runs after group fills in
Step-by-Step
1:30
(3 min) DS picks primary persona (highest-priority from Day 1). "We're mapping their journey end-to-end — from 'I have a need' to 'I'm done.'"
1:33
(5 min) Tech Lead displays Agent 10 draft journey scaffold (6–8 steps) — group reacts.
1:38
(15 min) Walk through each step. Group adds emotional context (frustrated/hopeful/confused) and pain points. Tech Lead photographs board.
1:53
(10 min) Identify the Golden Path — 3–5 steps representing core value. These MUST be supported by Must Have features.
2:03
(10 min) Agent 10 runs gap analysis: which journey steps have no Must Have feature? Group decides: add a feature, or accept the gap.
2:13
(7 min) DS confirms journey + gap resolutions. Updates Spec Matrix if gap fills became new Must Haves.
Facilitator Script
Opening
"Up to now we've been talking about features. Now let's talk about flow. We're going to map [Persona]'s actual journey — from the moment they have a need to the moment they're successful. Then we'll check: do our Must Haves cover this whole journey, or are there gaps?"
On Golden Path
"The Golden Path is the sequence of steps a user MUST be able to complete for this product to be considered successful. Every Golden Path step needs a Must Have feature behind it."
When gap surfaces
"Step [X] has no Must Have feature behind it. Two choices: add a Must, or accept that [step] won't work in v1. Which?"
Watch For · Recovery Moves
Journey is too generic
"Use specifics. What's the actual screen they see? What time of day? Who else is around?"
Group skips emotional context
"What does [persona] feel here? Naming the emotion is what design is for."
Golden Path is too long
"Five steps max. What's the absolute core?"
Gap analysis surfaces a big hole
"This is exactly why we mapped the journey. Better to find this now than in QA."
📦 Output: Validated journey map with Golden Path. Gap analysis report. Updated Spec Matrix if needed.

3:12

Facilitator Script
SA Opening
"My job in the next 50 minutes is to make sure what we've agreed to in MoSCoW is actually buildable in your timeline and budget. I'll lay out a recommended stack, walk through where we'd build vs. buy, and call out the technical risks I see. AI is drafting the Architecture Brief on the screen — feel free to interrupt anytime."
On build vs. buy
"Where it makes sense to use a third-party service — auth, payments, search — we should. It's cheaper, faster, and more reliable than building from scratch. Where the feature IS the differentiation, we build."
On risks
"Three risks I'm flagging: [list]. None of these are blockers. They're things we'll need to mitigate during the build."
Watch For · Recovery Moves
Stakeholder pushes back on tech choice
"Help me understand the concern — is it cost, lock-in, or familiarity? We can address each."
Group wants too much custom build
"Anything we build, we own forever. Buy where you can. Build where you must."
Compliance flag missed
SA: "Wait — if you're handling [data type], that triggers [HIPAA/PCI/GDPR]. That changes our database choice."
AI vendor suggestion is wrong
"AI flagged [vendor]. SA, your call — does this fit?" SA overrides as needed.
📦 Output: Draft Architecture Brief: tech stack, build vs. buy, NFR list, top 3 risks. SA reviews and approves same-day.
