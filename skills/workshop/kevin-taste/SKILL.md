# Kevin's Taste

Read on 2026-10-23 from about 520 of Kevin's own Cosmos saves (the "Ideas for" suggestions Cosmos appends
were excluded), the 21 Instagram references he sent, the four sites, and the DZNR OS decisions he made
about his own UI. His largest board is Tech (about 140 saves); his centre of gravity is
**instrument-grade objects**.

**Scope, stated first.** This profile is for work done *for Kevin*. An author's world follows
`author-world-authority`: the author is the director, and these principles may be offered to an author as
one option among the `style-library` entries, never applied by default. A client brand follows the
client. If you are unsure whose work it is, resolve it before loading this file (`legendary-standard`,
Step 0).

## The twelve principles

Each has what it means, an example from his boards or references, and a test an agent can run.

### 1. Instrument-grade objects
Every knob, scale, label and seam looks functional and manufacturable. He reveres Dieter Rams and Teenage
Engineering and enjoys "what if Sony made this in 1987" fiction.
- *Example:* TE OP-1 field, EP-133 with its grid spec sheet, a black e-ink device with one knob and a
  counter "00:04:14" (saved three times), a Braun TP1 radio-phono.
- *Test:* can you say what each control on the object does? If not, it fails.

### 2. One signal colour on neutrals
Aluminium, bone, graphite, Rams white and black, with **one** colour used as meaning: TE or safety orange
first, then alarm red, acid green, phosphor green. The same rule appears on hardware, gear, UI and
architecture (the one amber window).
- *Palette to start from:* aluminium #B9BCBF, bone #EDEAE3, graphite #2B2B2D, black #0B0B0C, signal
  orange #FF5A1F; alarm red #E8322A only for alarms.
- *Test:* count the hues outside the neutrals. More than one fails.

### 3. Material truth
Surfaces you could touch: bead-blasted and anodised aluminium, translucent and frosted polycarbonate,
knurling, membrane keys, speaker mesh, technical nylon, board-formed concrete, wet stone, walnut.
- *Example:* a translucent orange-and-white synth with knurled knobs on concrete (Unsorted board).
- *Test:* name the material of every visible surface. "Futuristic material" fails.

### 4. Speculative but grounded realism
The future must look photographed, not illustrated: real places (concrete aprons, hangars, cornfields),
documentary light, scuffs and peeling labels. @an_improbable_future is the model.
- *Test:* could this be an archive or press photograph of a real prototype? Glow or lens flare fails.

### 5. Interfaces as instruments
Monochrome hairline FUI, radial gauges, concentric rings with ticks, mono numerals, dot-matrix readouts,
data with purpose; Swiss order with sci-fi precision.
- *Example:* a voice recorder with a red concentric record button on black; a boarding pass in a
  grotesk and mono mix; Ledger Stax's HUD chrome.
- *Test:* does every number say what it measures? Decorative data fails.

### 6. Identity hidden behind engineered surfaces
Helmets, visors, balaclavas and technical shells with a fashion-editorial finish; the face recedes and the
gear tells the story.
- *Example:* a white full-face helmet with an orange visor; a Nike ACG topographic-quilted hood; @ovyonlabs
  ASEMIC's faceless figures.
- *Test:* are fastenings, seams and panels believable? Costume-shop sci-fi fails.

### 7. Body meets machine, never cute
Mature 90s cel-anime cyberpunk (*Ghost in the Shell*, *Akira*, *Patlabor* lineage) and *Ex Machina*:
cables, ports, dense hand-drawn line.
- *Test:* chibi proportions, moe faces or glossy modern anime rendering fail.

### 8. Monumental calm in places
Scale, silence, weather: brutalist and dark-minimal architecture, fog, a tiny human figure, wide
symmetrical or one-point framing, one warm light in a cold world.
- *Example:* a curved black wall carved into a sea cliff with one small figure; a glowing amber window
  in a rust monolith.
- *Test:* is there a human for scale and one warm source? Busy, saturated or crowded fails.

### 9. Heritage as a design language, without kitsch
1960s to 1990s industrial design remixed for now. No synthwave grid sunsets, no Y2K bubble chrome.
When his list names Synthwave or Y2K, use the sober variants in `style-library` (Y2K as translucent tech;
Synthwave as one sodium or magenta signal on near-black).
- *Test:* name the heritage anchor (Braun 1968, Sony 1987, tDR 1995). None, or a neon cliché, fails.

### 10. Quiet chrome, spectacle in the object
The UI and type stay quiet (a Helvetica-class grotesk, a mono, at most two faces) so one hero object or
world carries the frame. The animprobablefuture.com site uses one Helvetica Neue weight and lets the
objects bring the colour.
- *Test:* do the chrome and the hero compete? If the type is louder than the object, it fails.

### 11. A lens person's camera
He saves cine cameras, Leica and Rollei, engraved focus scales, a 75mm with an orange focus ring. Motion
should speak focal length, focus pulls and real optics; sound should be analogue and physical (reels,
detents, vinyl, tape), from his Sound board.
- *Test:* does a video brief name the lens, the distance and one camera move? "Cinematic" alone fails.

### 12. Depth, not outlines, in DZNR's own UI
In DZNR OS and Pulse OS chrome, cards, pills, tags and chips are separated by fill plus a soft two-layer
shadow, never by a 1px outline stroke (decision 218, 2026-10-14). Edges return only under Increase
Contrast or forced colours. Hairlines live *inside* instrument graphics (gauges, dials, reticles), not on
card edges. And figures explain themselves: the DZNR OS Ledger spend screen he likes gives every figure a
one-line meaning, a sentence of insight, and the basis of each cost.
- *Test:* any stroke-separated card in DZNR chrome fails `quality-gate` F13; a figure with no meaning line
  in a System view fails legibility.

## How to use this file

- **At Step 2 of `legendary-standard`** on DZNR's own work: this is the reference lock's taste source.
  Pick the `style-library` entries that fit (most often `instrument-grade-product`, `hud-instrument`,
  `grounded-speculative-realism`, `monumental-calm`, `techwear-editorial`, `swiss-design`).
- **In prompts:** turn principles into slots, never paste the adjectives. Principle 2 becomes a palette
  token with a hex; principle 11 becomes a lens and a move (`prompt-architecture`).
- **In the gate:** `quality-gate` reads this as the taste reference on DZNR's own work; the principle tests
  above are its evidence for originality and craft.
- **Examples worth loading:** `references/examples.md` lists the saves and references behind each
  principle, with the creators credited.

## What Kevin does not want (from the same evidence)

- Generic AI cyberpunk with mush in the background (Neon Orian scored 3 on originality for this).
- Dark mode plus lime plus iridescent glass as a reflex (hyliox executes it well; it is now a trope).
- Decorative gradients, purple AI glow, emoji, outline-stroked cards in his product UI.
- Spectacle without meaning (the "AI slop" lesson from @atsmatrix's comments).
- Chibi, moe, synthwave sunsets, Y2K bubbles.
